Interior lighting

Light the room, not just the middle of the ceiling

One fixture in the centre of a room leaves the corners, the counters and the reading chair in shadow. Pot lights spread the light where you actually use it.

LuMar Electric installs pot lights and interior lighting for homeowners in Moncton and surrounding areas and across Prince Edward Island — in a kitchen, a living room, a basement being finished, or a whole renovation.

The photo is one of the suites at The Village at Jonathan Park in Moncton, where LuMar did the full electrical. A good layout starts with how you use the room: where you cook, where you read, and which lights you want on the same switch.

Where people add them

  • Kitchens, over the counters and the island
  • Living rooms and family rooms with a single ceiling fixture
  • Basements being finished
  • Hallways, stairs and entrances
  • Bathrooms
  • Soffits and outside entrances

Adding a lot of new lighting to an older house? It is worth checking the panel has room for it; our panel upgrade page explains why. For lighting you control from your phone, or scenes for a whole floor, see SmartSpace. For the outside of the house, see permanent outdoor lighting.

Questions

What people ask us

Don’t see yours? Call the office and ask a licensed electrician.

Can pot lights go into a finished ceiling?

Often, yes. Tell us whether your ceilings are open or finished when you get in touch, and we’ll look at what’s above the ceiling before we quote.

Which rooms do people add them to?

Kitchens, living and family rooms, basements being finished, hallways and stairs, bathrooms, and soffits and outside entrances.

Can I control them from my phone?

Yes. For lighting you control from your phone, or scenes for a whole floor, see SmartSpace.

Will my panel handle more lighting?

In an older house it’s worth checking. Our panel upgrade page explains why, and we can look at both on the same visit.

Plan the light around the room

If it waits

  • Rooms lit from one point, dark at the edges
  • Lights on the wrong switches
  • Adding a lot of lighting to an older house without checking the panel

When it’s done right

  • Light where you cook, read and work
  • A layout planned around how you use the room
  • Energy-efficient LED fixtures
  • Done cleanly by licensed electricians
